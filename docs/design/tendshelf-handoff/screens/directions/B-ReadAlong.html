The Second Lamp

The second lamp had not been lit in eleven winters. Nell found it where her father always said it would be, wrapped in oilcloth beneath the stairs, its brass gone the soft brown of an old coin.

She carried it up the hundred and nine steps with both hands, the way you carry something that might still be asleep. At the top the wind was waiting. It leaned its whole weight on the glass and hummed through the frame, a low note that never quite resolved.

“You’ll want the long matches,” said a voice behind her, and she did not turn around, because she already knew whose it was.
